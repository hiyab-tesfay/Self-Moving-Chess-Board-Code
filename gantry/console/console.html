The board (Sketch 5)

Check with the camera

The camera only checks: it never moves the gantry to correct anything and never changes the calibration. 1) Home first. 2) Clear the board. 3) Press Check with camera. 4) When asked, make sure the board is completely empty (hands out of view) and press Done: the camera photographs it. 5) Put one chess piece (a pawn or a rook) anywhere on the board and press Done. The camera finds the piece, the magnet picks it up and carries it to where the gantry thinks each square below is (a8, h8, h1, a1, then d5, e4); the camera measures how far off the real centre it lands. Within 1.5 mm everywhere: the board is calibrated. Further off: fix it with Tune (nudge by eye, Right place) or this square's centre is here, then check again. The piece is left on the last square. Set camera_cal_piece_height_mm in gantry_config.json to your piece's height; the printed token is optional (camera_cal_target "token").

Tune the squares

Pick Tune above the moves (or press a square here), click a square on the board: the magnet goes where the gantry thinks it is. Nudge it onto the square's real centre, then press Right place. Every answer is kept and the rest of the board follows: one square shifts everything, a few far apart (a1, h1, h8, a8, then the middle) also fix spacing and tilt. Saved in gantry_config.json at once.

Speeds

Stalls and buzzing

A stepper buzzes without turning when it is asked for more than it can give at that speed, when it gets less current or voltage than it needs, or when the carriage binds. These checks tell which.
1. One motor turn
Each button sends exactly one motor turn of steps on that axis (200 full steps). On the bench one turn moves the carriage 41 mm. Measure how far it went and the page saves it as that axis's mm per turn, which sets how the board counts. + is away from home (the bottom-left corner), − is toward it. No limits: it runs anywhere, homed or not, even at the edge, so keep a hand near Stop.
2. Distance check (a long run, for accuracy)
Moves one axis a long way (300 mm is good) at 10 mm/s, as the board counts it. Measure with a tape how far it really went: over 300 mm a 1 mm error is 0.3 %, far better than one turn. The page works out that axis's mm per turn.
mm counted
3. Speed ladder (how fast it can go)
Goes out and back at rising speeds. Watch and listen: press It stalled the moment it buzzes, skips or stops. The page then suggests speeds with a margin. Home it first; a stall loses the position.
mm mm/s
4. Push test (binding)
Press Motors off, then push the carriage slowly along X across the whole board, then along Y. It should glide with an even, light force. A tight spot is binding (a V-wheel too tight, the beam twisting because only one side is driven, a belt rubbing): the motor will stall there at any speed.
5. Power and current
  • Motor supply at the driver's VMOT must stay at 12 V or more while moving. The DRV8825 shuts off below 8.2 V; a battery pack sags under load, which shows as stalls at speed and as the ESP32 restarting by itself.
  • Vref sets the current: current limit = 2 × Vref. For a 13 N·cm NEMA17 rated about 1 A, set Vref about 0.4 to 0.5 V. Too low gives buzzing under load; too high makes the driver overheat and cut out in bursts (give it a heatsink).
  • A motor that only buzzes even at 5 mm/s has a loose or swapped coil wire: its two coil pairs must each go to one driver output pair (1A/1B and 2A/2B).
